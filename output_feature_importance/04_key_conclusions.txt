====================================================================================================
CONCLUSIONS SUPPORTED BY DATA
====================================================================================================

1. GLOBAL DENSITY MEASURES (Histogram Entropy, Mean Lung Density):
   ✓ Show strongest univariate correlations with FVC (|r| ≈ 0.59-0.68)
   ✓ Highest individual predictive power (R² ≈ 0.31-0.41)
   ✗ BUT exhibit high variability across datasets (CV ≈ 0.34-0.40)
   → Interpretation: Sample-dependent, less generalizable patterns

2. PERIPHERAL STRUCTURE MEASURES (Volume, Density, Diameter):
   ✓ Show weaker univariate correlations (|r| ≈ 0.17-0.47)
   ✓ BUT demonstrate SUPERIOR stability across datasets (CV ≈ 0.07-0.14)
   ✓ More robust and reliable patterns
   → Interpretation: Trade-off between weak univariate signal and high robustness

3. CENTRAL-TO-PERIPHERAL DIAMETER RATIO:
   ✓ Consistent moderate correlation (|r| ≈ 0.30)
   ✓ Stable across datasets (CV ≈ 0.29)
   ✓ Reliable but lower-magnitude predictor

4. THE CENTRAL PARADOX:
   • Global density measures show STRONG univariate correlation with FVC (r ≈ 0.64)
   • Yet FAIL to translate into robust multivariate predictive power
   • Without baseline FVC information, single-feature models achieve R² ≈ 0.31-0.41
   • This pattern reveals:
      - The importance of feature combinations and ensemble effects
      - Why robust, stable features matter more than high univariate correlation
      - Fundamental limits of univariate signal in small-sample multivariate prediction

KEY TAKEAWAY:
While global density measures display stronger individual correlations with FVC decline,
peripheral structure measurements (volume, density, diameter) offer superior generalizability
and robustness across different dataset configurations. This suggests that for practical
clinical prediction, stable features with moderate univariate signal are preferable to
strong but unstable features, particularly in small-sample scenarios.
